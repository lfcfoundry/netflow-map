# Changelog

Copyright (c) 2026 LFC Foundry. MIT License.

What changed in Netflow Map, in plain words, newest first. Developer notes, in three levels of detail, are in docs/CHANGELOG-DEV.md. Each build is stamped `year.month.day.HHMM` (US Eastern) in the page header. This is the short list for people who use the app. The long version for each build (every measurement, test count and tool change) is kept in the private knowledge base, under `projects/netflow-map/changelogs/`.

Tested one browser at a time in Firefox 158.0, ungoogled-chromium 155.0.8059.39 and WebKitGTK 2.52.6. Not tested for any build: Safari itself, any iPhone, Chrome on a phone.

## November 1

- **2026.11.01.1126** Changing colors or labels on the nested and round views is quicker on big maps. At 100,000 boxes, building the new picture takes about half the time it did in Firefox and WebKitGTK, and 5 to 40 percent less in Chromium (tidy tree 1.3 seconds to 0.76). The pictures are exactly the same.
- **2026.11.01.1059** Changing colors, labels or label text on a big map is quicker when the browser draws with the graphics card: the app no longer prepares the old picture for the card a second time before the new one. On a 100,000-box treemap a color change went from about 4 seconds to 1.2 to 1.4 seconds of page time in Chromium, and the tidy tree from 4.2 to 3.3 seconds. The picture is exactly the same.
- **2026.11.01.1045** The Charts tab no longer offers a pie chart; it has 22 types now. A saved pie chart opens as a donut, which shows the same shares with the total in the middle. Map views are unchanged: the nested and round views now come from two shared modules (netflow-hierarchy and netflow-circular) and draw exactly the same pictures.
- **2026.11.01.1003** Seven new map views and a new Charts tab. The new views are Treemap, Icicle, Sunburst, Circle packing, Tidy tree, Bundled links and Chord. They nest items by their links when the links form a tree, by group otherwise, or by a parent column (Nest by picks), and they never let boxes, circles or tree rows touch. The Charts tab draws 23 plain charts from the item or link columns: bars, lines, areas, scatter, connected scatter, beeswarm, dot plot, histogram, box plot, density, ridgeline, hexbin, heat map or calendar, pie, donut, radial bars, parallel lines, a scatter grid, Q-Q, slope, Marimekko, Voronoi and a word cloud. Each picks sensible columns, names its colors in a key row, shows the row under the pointer and saves as SVG. Nothing changes in maps you already have. The Chord view has a new Order setting: Fastest, the default, keeps items in group order; Fewest crossings searches the order around the circle so fewer bands cross. The treemap, icicle, circle packing and tree views lay out faster than d3-hierarchy, a popular chart toolkit, in all three test browsers at 10,000 and 100,000 items (0.29 to 0.89 of its time), and at 1,000 items in Chromium and Firefox; in WebKitGTK at 1,000 items the icicle view ties. Chord with Fastest is about as quick as d3-chord in Firefox and 2 to 3.5 times quicker in Chromium and WebKitGTK; Fewest crossings takes longer than d3-chord (under 2 ms at 60 items) and crosses up to 53% less (lab).
- **2026.11.01.0219** Flow and top-down maps are laid out a little faster on big maps, with exactly the same pictures (the layout module netflow-layers moved to 1.9.0). At 1,200 items Flow plus top-down took 0.81 to 0.85 of the time in Chromium, 0.86 in Firefox and 0.92 to 0.95 in WebKitGTK (lab).
- **2026.11.01.0154** The light/dark button is quicker when the Map is not on screen: it no longer redraws the hidden map at once, only when you open the Map or save a picture. Switching on another tab with a 20,000-item map went from about half a second to a few hundredths in Chromium (Firefox about 30 times quicker at 5,000 items, WebKitGTK about a third quicker). The map looks the same.
- **2026.11.01.0017** Flow, top-down, spine and lanes views draw small maps with fewer crossing links. On maps under 500 boxes, Auto now also tries the harder untangling and keeps whichever picture crosses less, so it is never messier than before. On a 400-item test map links cross 4,663 times instead of 5,351, fewer than dagre (5,374) and ELK (5,313), two popular layout tools; on 40 imported tables under 500 boxes, 3.7% fewer crossings. These maps take about 2 to 2.7 times as long to lay out (400 items: 31-37 to 82-91 ms, lab), still about 9 times quicker than those tools. Turn it off by picking Deeper in Crossing search.
- **2026.11.01.0011** The Lighter crossing search (used by Auto on maps of 800 boxes or more) now looks a little longer before it stops, so it draws the same tidy pictures as Deeper: on 55 test maps it never leaves extra crossings any more (before, 7 of them had 2 to 7% more). Big maps take a bit longer again: a 1,000-item map draws in about 117 ms in Chromium, 126 in Firefox and 125 in WebKitGTK, against 74, 85 and 86 ms in the last build (lab). Smaller maps are the same.

## October 31

- **2026.10.31.2339** New setting: Crossing search (Auto, Deeper, Lighter) in the Flow, top-down, spine and lanes views. Deeper is the tidiest. Lighter is quicker on big maps and now and then leaves a few more crossing links. Auto, the default, uses Deeper below 800 boxes and Lighter from there. A 1,000-item map now draws in 74 instead of 115 ms in Chromium, 85 instead of 122 in Firefox and 86 instead of 123 in WebKitGTK (lab), with the same crossings. Maps under 800 boxes look exactly as before.
- **2026.10.31.2310** The Sankey view is drawn a little faster again, with the same picture. Work that only folded views need (finding items in a single line) and only lane views need is skipped. At 1,500 items: Chromium 10.3 to 9.3 ms, Firefox 11.0 to 10.2 ms, WebKitGTK 12.0 to 11.4 ms; at 400 items 3-14% faster (lab).
- **2026.10.31.2257** The page takes less memory and opens a little faster in Chromium, with nothing you can see changed. The app's file held 30 characters (an arrow, a euro sign, a few letter ranges) that made the browser keep the whole page's text at twice the size. They are now written as codes that mean the same character. In a lab run, Chromium's memory for the page went from 34.4 to 31.3 MB and loading from 160 to 133 ms; Firefox and WebKitGTK are the same.
- **2026.10.31.2253** Undo keeps more steps after a change to every item (or every link), such as setting one group for all. Such a change now keeps only the list it changed, so the last 25 can be undone where before only the last 9 could (3,000-row map, lab). Each change holds the page the same time or less in all three test browsers.
- **2026.10.31.2214** The Sankey view draws big maps 2 times faster. On maps of 1,000 items or more it keeps at most 500 hidden stops, and the lightest long links are drawn straight, the way d3-sankey draws all of them. At 1,500 items it takes 12-13 ms against d3-sankey's 23-27 ms in all three test browsers, and 256 band pieces pass over other bars against d3-sankey's 1,916. Maps under 1,000 items look exactly as before. The Sankey layout also keeps its hidden stops in compact number lists, with the same picture.
- **2026.10.31.2205** Renaming, grouping or marking items on a big table holds the page for less time again, with nothing you can see changed. With a 16 MB table open and the Data tab shown, each rename held the page 29 to 11 ms in Chromium, 29 to 5 ms in Firefox and 27 to 14 ms in WebKitGTK (lab). The column summary no longer reads a column again just because it has fewer than three different values (an unused label column, for example), the check for whether the data is a story reads the summary instead of every item, and the count of placeholders is kept up instead of counted again. Small maps are the same or faster.
- **2026.10.31.2045** The Sankey view, and coloring by the odds of each ending, work out where flow ends faster, with the same answers. On a map with no loops one pass is now exact, and a second run of the same flow spread is gone. At 1,500 items that step went from about 2.7 to 0.9 ms (lab).
- **2026.10.31.2031** Renaming, grouping or marking items on a big table holds the page for less time again, with nothing you can see changed. With a 16 MB table open, each such change held the page 36 to 28 ms in Chromium, 35 to 23 ms in Firefox and 27 to 14 ms in WebKitGTK (lab). A change to items no longer copies and compares the 563,000 links. Small maps are the same or faster.
- **2026.10.31.2021** Flow, top-down and spine views cross fewer links, and the Sankey is drawn faster. A deeper search for the order of each row now runs on every map up to 20,000 stops, and the new Rows choice Fewest crossings (now the default) tries both row rules and keeps the tidier one. At 1,000 items links cross 21,812 times, fewer than dagre (23,965) and ELK (22,602), two popular layout tools; small maps only ever get tidier. The Sankey skips a layout step it never used: 1,500 items take 36 to 29 ms in Firefox and 39 to 28 ms in WebKitGTK (lab), with the same picture.
- **2026.10.31.2021** In the Sankey view, a start item with no links out used to leave every item stacked in one empty column. Now the bands show the link weights, and Why this view says so and suggests picking another start.
- **2026.10.31.1948** Tables open faster, most of all in WebKit browsers. Reading a 16 MB table (563,563 rows) took 390 to 119 ms in WebKitGTK and 229 to 167 ms in Chromium (lab); Firefox is the same. Netflow Map now reads tables faster than Papa Parse and d3-dsv, two popular table readers, in all three test browsers. The rows read are exactly the same.
- **2026.10.31.1830** Each change on a big table holds the page about a quarter as long again, with nothing you can see changed. With a 16 MB table open and the Data tab shown, the page was held after a rename for 138 to 42 ms in Chromium, 82 to 22 ms in Firefox and 131 to 37 ms in WebKitGTK (lab). Each change used to compare the whole map before and after up to three times, look up every item by name only to count the ones with no way on, and build 50,000 bits of text to see whether the table might hold states. Now the comparison is done once, the count is read from the app's own link list, and the states check runs again only when links change ends. Small maps got faster too (20 changes on a 6,000-item map: 0.37 to 0.69 of the time before).
- **2026.10.31.1815** Numbers shown in the app (map labels, totals, the report and the panels) are written out up to 20 times faster, with exactly the same text: 10,000 numbers took 172 to 10 ms in Chromium, 78 to 4 ms in Firefox and 261 to 11 ms in WebKitGTK (lab).
- **2026.10.31.1750** Your saved map now lives in the browser's own database instead of the small save space that every app on this site shares. With a big map saved, that space held up to 1.8 MB of it, about a third of what all the apps together may keep there, and in Chromium every other app's start paid for reading it (about 17 ms in a lab run). Now only a tiny note stays there (1.3 KB in a lab run, down from 1.74 MB). Saving a big map holds the page less: 78 to 4 ms over five saves in Chromium, 37 to 4 ms in Firefox (WebKitGTK the same). Opening Netflow Map with a big saved map: Chromium a little faster (87 to 82 ms to the first screen), Firefox and WebKitGTK about 20 ms slower. Maps saved by an earlier version are moved over the first time you open this one. A change made right before closing the page is still kept.
- **2026.10.31.1730** Each change on a big table freezes the page much less again, with nothing you can see changed. The summary of what each column holds (numbers, dates, groups) is now updated for the changed items only, instead of read again from every cell. With a 16 MB table open and the Data tab shown, the page was held after a rename for 262 to 138 ms in Chromium, 292 to 91 ms in Firefox and 432 to 146 ms in WebKitGTK. Small maps are unchanged.
- **2026.10.31.1645** On very sharp phone screens (pixel ratio above 2), the map is drawn at 2 times the screen's size in points instead of up to 2.5, as the games already do. At ratio 3 in a phone-sized window that is 36% fewer pixels and less canvas memory (3.92 to 2.52 million pixels), and about 14% less drawing work while panning (lab). Lines are a little less crisp on those screens; screens at ratio 2 or below are unchanged.
- **2026.10.31.1603** On a big map, coming back to it after a short rest no longer freezes the page. To free memory, the app ends its layout helper 4 s after a big map's last change, and the next change starts a new one. Before, that restart copied the whole map on the page: with a 16 MB table, a freeze of 0.6 to 0.8 s in Chromium, 0.8 to 1.2 s in Firefox and 1.6 to 1.8 s in WebKitGTK. Now the resting helper's map is packed small on a thread of its own, and the new helper reads it from there: the page is held 8 to 32 ms. The restart itself takes about 0.1 to 0.6 s longer.
- **2026.10.31.1440** Changes on a big map freeze the page much less again, with nothing you can see changed. With a 16 MB table open, the longest freeze after a change went from 0.85 to 0.27 s in Chromium, 0.83 to 0.39 s in Firefox and 1.13 to 0.46 s in WebKitGTK (same-session lab runs). Before, the app built its list of every item and link again after each change; now it updates only the items that changed. The check that looks for numbers in each column also skips text that can't be a number (most names and labels) at its first letter. Small maps got faster too, not slower.
- **2026.10.31.1410** Changes on a big map freeze the page much less, with nothing you can see changed. With a 16 MB table open, the longest freeze after a change went from 1.5 to 0.9 s in Chromium, 1.8 to 1.0 s in Firefox and 2.6 to 1.5 s in WebKitGTK. Before, every change copied the whole map to the layout helper on the page itself (1.8 s in Chromium); now only what changed goes (20 ms). The autosave also wrote the whole map out (61 MB of text) after every change only to find it too big to keep; it now stops as soon as it passes the limit (about 400 ms to about 10 ms). Opening a big JSON map reads the file once instead of twice: the 30,000-item test map holds the page about 14% less in Chromium and WebKitGTK and 9% less in Firefox.
- **2026.10.31.1200** Your changes now survive a browser crash, and opening a second big file takes much less memory. Every save is also kept in the browser's database, asked to reach the disk at once; before, a crash or a hard close could lose the last minute of changes in Chromium and the last 10 s in Firefox. When the app opens with a newer copy there, it brings it back and tells you. In the lab, killed 2 s or 30 s after a change, the change was lost every time before and kept every time now. Also: the map from before you open a file is now packed small for Undo, and all of Undo is held to 16 MB (past that, the oldest steps go). With a 16 MB table, after a second table and 24 changes, the page held 166 MB and now 108 MB (a table with more text: 58 to 44 MB). Undoing back to the earlier file takes a little longer: up to 2.8 s at 188,000 items, against 1.9 to 2.7 s for the slowest undo before.
- **2026.10.31.0958** Big maps take much less memory, with nothing you can see changed. With a 16 MB table open, after 12 changes, a second 16 MB table and 12 more changes, the page held 287 MB before and 166 MB now (a table with more text in it: 116 MB to 58 MB). Undo keeps only what each change replaced instead of the whole map, so all 25 steps still work. The cells of an opened table, kept for "Change how this table is read", are now read again from the file's text when you use it. Text that repeats in the data (a label, a group, a kind) is kept once while the page is idle.
- **2026.10.31.0925** Network maps made of several separate pieces lay out about 2 times faster on computers with more than one core (up to 2.5 times): each piece of 150 to 4,000 items is laid out on its own helper thread, up to 3. Three pieces of 3,000 items took 3.3 s and now take 1.4 s in Chromium (3.8 to 1.5 s in Firefox, 4.0 to 1.7 s in WebKitGTK). The picture is exactly the same. Maps of one piece, or of a few small pieces, are laid out as before. The helpers end 30 s after their last piece, which gives their memory back.
- **2026.10.31.0846** Fixed: after opening a second network map of 1,500 items or more with the same number of items as the first, in the same session, the app could show the first map's places instead of laying out the new one (since 2026.10.31.0826). Pictures saved in the browser since then are no longer used, so big maps are laid out once more.
- **2026.10.31.0826** Changing a color, link color or link width on a big network map is now 1.6 to 3.2 times faster: at 100,000 items, a new link color took 6 to 7 s and now takes 2.2 to 2.8 s. Before, any such change laid the whole map out again, even though nothing moves, and saved one more copy of the same picture in the browser. Now the finished picture is kept and put back. The picture is exactly the same and the first layout takes as long as before. Also: handing a finished map from the background worker to the page is faster, and a build makes about a fifth less garbage for the browser to clean up.

Item places are now kept as whole numbers, which moves an item at most half a unit, less than a pixel. Map files saved before this still open exactly as before. Items now move a few pixels on maps of every size, because the push-apart step walks the map in grid order. Also fixed: JSON Lines files (.jsonl and .ndjson, one record a line) opened as a table of text instead of as links; they now open as the map they describe. Every file type the app takes (23 kinds, plus saved .json.lfc map files) is now opened through the file picker in every browser test. The README and landing page screenshots are in the dark theme.
- **2026.10.30.2233** Network maps over 80,000 items lay out faster the first time. When the middle of the map starts out very crowded, it is now stretched once before items are pushed apart, not after 10 rounds of pushing that barely helped. At 100,000 items the first layout took 4.6 to 4.1 s in Firefox and 4.2 to 3.8 s in WebKitGTK (Chromium about the same, within its noise). The page is the same size, the middle sits a little tighter but more evenly spaced, and still nothing touches. Smaller maps are unchanged.
- **2026.10.30.2131** Nothing you can see changes: the shared compression module that packs a big map's links while you leave it alone is now version 2.6.6 from JS-modules. It packs the same bytes, and packing and waking a map are faster (on 100,000 links, waking took 204 to 66 ms in Chromium and 522 to 277 ms in Firefox).
- **2026.10.30.2122** Network maps over 50,000 items lay out about a quarter faster the first time: the step that pushes touching items apart does 10 rounds instead of 30. The picture moves very slightly, with the same truth score, the same page size and still nothing touching. Smaller maps are unchanged.
- **2026.10.30.2018** Big network maps lay out faster the first time, with exactly the same picture: 100,000 items about 23% faster in Chromium and 13% in Firefox, and 30,000 items about 32% faster in WebKitGTK. The small copy of the map that the layout starts from now works out its hop counts with far fewer steps, and each item's size is worked out once instead of about ten times.
- **2026.10.30.1817** Big 3D maps with dotted links turn much faster. Dotted links that are zoomed so far out that the dots are under 2 pixels apart now draw as one thin solid line with the same average ink, and the dots come back as soon as you zoom in. In a lab run on 2,000 items, drawing a picture went from about 280 ms to about 3 ms, and the picture differed in 0.01% of its pixels.

## October 26

- **2026.10.26.2330** Faster in Firefox: WebM video now encodes in the browser's realtime mode at twice the bit rate, about 5 times faster (the file is larger, and as close to the original picture as before). Other browsers are unchanged. On the graphics card path, up to 8 pictures are sent up in one call.
- **2026.10.26.1627** New: a grouped SVG for big maps. The Export tab now has "SVG (grouped, small)" and "SVG (every item its own element)". The grouped one puts links, items and labels with the same look into one group: the 30,000-item map with 94,597 links goes from 17.5 MB and about 127,000 elements to 7.0 MB and about 30,000 elements (links become 69 paths), keeps the 150 best connected items on maps over 5,000 items and sets their names large enough to read with the whole map on screen (no overlapping ones; the full export keeps 2,500 specks), has no background (it goes onto any page; text color follows the app theme) and has no fixed size so a browser fits the whole map to the window. Stacking of links among links may differ slightly from the full one. Also: a map file now shows how long it took to open (Data tab) and to save (Export tab).
- **2026.10.26.1346** New: save a map as one small file and open it again. On the Export tab, Data card, "Map file (.json.lfc)" saves the items, links, states, view settings and, when the browser kept it, the finished picture (LFC-pack, the new file format). Drop the file on the Data tab to open it: nothing is laid out again when the picture is inside. The 30,000-item map with 90,029 links is 1.09 MB as a map file against 8.9 MB of data as text; saving took about 0.5 s (Chromium 155) and opening to a shown map took 2.9 s against 4.8 s the first time (WebKitGTK 2.52.6: 10.6 s against 12.5 s). Start-up is not slowed by more than about 4%, because the file module (about 160 KB packed, 666 KB unpacked) is only unpacked the first time a map file is saved or opened. File format may still change before the first release.
- **2026.10.26.1320** Dragging along the timeline (or playing it) on a big map is 2 to 6 times faster per step. Each step used to build a copy of every item and link; now the copies are made once and only the numbers that differ between two states are updated. On the 30,000-item map (90,029 links) one step took 328, 220 and 177 ms before and takes 101, 37 and 40 ms in Chromium 155, Firefox 158 and WebKitGTK 2.52.6 (lower is better). The pictures are identical (12 steps compared exactly). The GIF and video maker still builds each picture the old way.
- **2026.10.26.0030** Selecting a lot of items with the box or lasso no longer freezes the page. Before, the page searched the whole map once for every selected item, so a box over all 30,000 items of a big map took 12.8 seconds in Chromium 155; now it takes 6 milliseconds and picks exactly the same items. Maps of a few hundred items were already instant and look the same.
- **2026.10.26.0001** The GIF and video maker now draws the pictures of a 3D turn of a big map (5,000 items or more, 20 pictures or more, 4 or more processor cores) in 2 to 4 background workers, instead of one picture at a time on the page. The files come out byte for byte the same. On the 30,000-item test map, 60 pictures at 960 wide, on a 4-core test machine (lower is better): Chromium 64.1 s down to 30 to 33 s; Firefox 56 s down to 50 s (more cores help more, and your laptop test page measured 3.9 ms a picture in 4 workers against 9.9 ms on the page). The key and caption are still drawn on the page, because text drawn in a worker differed slightly in Firefox. New tests: four byte-for-byte GIF checks and one check that a picture drawn with no page matches the page's.

## October 25

- **2026.10.25.2230** Big maps use less memory: the lists of links going in and out of each item are now two number lists instead of one list of objects per item (30,000 items, 94,597 links: whole page memory 18.4 to 12.5 MB in Chromium). Finding the neighbors of an item and the routes through it is faster too (routes from 5 items 320 to 93 ms in Chromium). New tests: the same links in the same order as before, and the same neighbors and routes.
- **2026.10.25.2125** Fixed two video file bugs: some browsers (Firefox) refused the MP4 because three header flags were written in the wrong place, and the seek points in longer WebM videos were a few bytes off. New test for both.
- **2026.10.25.2110** Fixed the map going black after a change on a big 3D map (for example Look, Arrangement, Distances 3D (stress) on 30,000 items): the graphics card's pre-load drew into the picture the browser was showing and left it empty until you pressed Fit. The pre-load now draws into a picture of its own.
- **2026.10.25.1326** The GIF maker can now save a video instead: new "File type" choice (GIF, or Video, which is MP4 where your browser can make it and WebM otherwise; if it can make neither you get a GIF and are told). On a busy 30,000-item map a video came out at about 0.6 MB per 100 pictures against about 5 MB for the GIF (lower is better). A new line under the choices says how many pictures and about how big the file will be before you start. The legend has a new "Legend" choice: Short (the default: the first six groups, then "+ N more", with the size, line width and marker lines in a smaller block), Full (every group) or Off, and the old "Include the key" box is gone (an old Off setting carries over). The video plays in the page (it shows its first picture until you press play), and you can save it. The page's security rules gained one line for this, `media-src blob:`: only files made in the page itself.
- **2026.10.25.1245** The key (legend) on big maps is tidier: when it has many rows it uses up to three columns and ends with a "+ N more" line instead of one very tall column. New "Where the key goes" choice for GIFs (bottom left, bottom right, top left, top right). Keys with few rows look the same as before.
- **2026.10.25.1237** New "File size" choice for GIFs (Best picture, Smaller file, Smallest file; Smaller is the new default). On a busy 30,000-item map the file went from 6.9 MB to about 5.3 MB (lower is better) with Smaller and about 4 MB with Smallest. Turning the other way after each pause now also turns the other way at the pause where the GIF starts over (an odd number of turns is rounded up to even).
- **2026.10.25.1218** The GIF maker draws its pictures on a canvas kept in memory instead of one held on the graphics card. On one laptop drawing a picture took 21 ms instead of 103 ms (lower is better), so GIFs finish much sooner. The map on screen is not changed.
