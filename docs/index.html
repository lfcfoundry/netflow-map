What it is and how it works

Netflow Map is a free tool for seeing and analyzing how things connect and flow. It works with any kind of linked data: links and nodes, edges and vertices, pages and choices.

How it works:

  1. Read: tables, Excel, JSON, outlines, Markdown notes, graph files, or a zip of them.
  2. Lay out: sixteen views, each answering a different question, with no two items touching, plus 22 plain charts from your columns.
  3. Analyze: find choke points, check the data, and simulate how flows spread.
  4. Share: export pictures, data, an animated GIF or a one-page report.

It is one file that runs in your browser. Nothing is uploaded or installed.

Why it exists

Data about connections usually lives in lists, and a list hides its shape: what everything depends on, where flows pile up, which groups barely touch. Netflow Map draws that shape quickly and clearly, so the signal stands out from the noise, even at 100,000 items.

It runs in the browser because almost every device has one. So it works offline and on locked-down computers, and your data stays with you.

Where it helps:

  • Software systems: which services depend on which.
  • Processes: where work waits, and which steps every job must pass.
  • Supply chains and money flows: how routes shift over time.
  • Linked notes and wikis: hubs, clusters and links to missing notes.
  • Branching stories: which endings people reach, and how often.
  • Research networks: citations, co-authors and other edge lists.

Highlights

Sixteen views

Flow, top-down, choke points, lanes, shape, Sankey, arc, matrix and network, plus treemap, icicle, sunburst, circle packing, tidy tree, bundled links and chord.

22 plain charts

Bars, lines, areas, scatter, box plots, histograms, ridgelines, hexbins, calendars, donuts, Q-Q, slope, Marimekko, Voronoi, word clouds and more, each picking sensible columns on its own.

No two items touch

Every network arrangement ends with a step that guarantees it, and a test checks it.

Reads almost anything

CSV, JSON, outlines, Excel, GraphML, GEXF, DOT, zips and Markdown notes. All 23 file kinds are opened in every browser test.

Simulation

Up to 100,000 walkers with exact odds, error bars and what-if ranking.

Items touching, 10,000 items

At 10,000 items Netflow Map was 42 times faster than the fastest library in Chromium, 35 in Firefox 158 and 30 in WebKitGTK 2.52.6. Every layout is drawn with Netflow Map's item sizes; the libraries place points, so they are first scaled until their middle link is as long as ours. Netflow Map's time is the whole build (analysis, layout, and the step that keeps items apart). Cytoscape.js was too slow to run above 1,000 items.

Links drawn over other bars

Netflow Map is about 2 times faster in every browser and routes long links around the bars, so far fewer pass over a bar they do not belong to. Maps under 1,000 items route every link; at 400 items Netflow Map takes 5-9 ms against 8-12 and crosses 23% fewer links. It also handles loops, which d3-sankey refuses.

Built for big maps

Measured on a map of 188,000 items and 563,000 links in a small 800 by 600 window.

Edits stay smooth

Rename an item and the page is busy for about 40 ms in Chromium, 22 ms in Firefox and 37 ms in WebKitGTK, well under a blink.

No freeze when it wakes

After a rest, the layout helper wakes without holding the page: about 20 ms in Chromium and Firefox, 32 ms in WebKitGTK.

Saves that survive a crash

Every change is also written to the browser's own database and sent to disk at once. Kill the browser two seconds after a change and the change is still there when you reopen it.

Leaves room for other apps

A big saved map lives in that database, not in the small save space every page on a site shares: 1.3 KB there instead of 1.7 MB.
